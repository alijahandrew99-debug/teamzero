## Correction — RingCentral AIR
Re-scraped ringcentral.com/air.html and ringcentral.com/pricing/ai-receptionist.html directly this week (last week's figures came from a third-party source, not the official page). Actual structure: **Standalone $49/mo, 100 min included, $0.50/min overage, billed in 30-second increments** — explicitly sold without any RingEX phone subscription required — or **Bundle: $39/mo AIR + $30/mo RingEX Core = $69/mo total**, also 100 min. ## Recommendations (proposals only)
1. **Update the RingCentral sales line.** Last week's framing — "AIR is a cheap-looking add-on that secretly requires buying their phone system" — is no longer accurate; AIR is standalone. A more durable comparison: AIR's $49/100min list price is $0.49/min nominal, but its $0.50/min overage after 100 min means a customer doing real volume (Front Desk's 1,000 min/mo) would pay roughly $499/mo on RingCentral's overage pricing alone, with no lead-gen — worth having this specific math ready rather than the "bundle-locked" line, which a prospect could now disprove with one look at RingCentral's own page.
2. **Dialzara now spans $29 to $1,500+/mo**, bracketing Front Desk's $399 from both sides. Since none of Dialzara's tiers, including the $1,500+ managed one, include lead-gen, this is a clean angle for marketing copy or sales conversations: "a managed AI answering line costs as much as our entire plan, and still doesn't send you leads."
