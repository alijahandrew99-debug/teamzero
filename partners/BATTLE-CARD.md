# Battle card — "Isn't there something cheaper out there?"

Yes, there is. Say so. Then win anyway. This page is how.

Prices below were verified against competitor sites in Aug 2026; re-check
monthly, and never quote a competitor figure you haven't seen yourself this
month.

---

## The honest problem, and the honest answer

**The problem:** on a raw per-minute comparison we lose. Rosie sells 1,000
minutes for $149; we sell 500 for $499. A prospect who shops on minutes will
pick Rosie, and a rep who argues per-minute math will lose that argument,
because it's true.

**The answer: never sell the minute. Sell the front desk.**

Every competitor on that table does *half* the job — the defensive half. They
stop you losing the customers who already call. **Nobody else also goes and
finds the next customer.** Dawnpipe answers the phone AND, between calls,
finds 200 businesses that match who you sell to, writes each a personal
email, and sends it when you approve. That's not a feature difference; it's a
different category. The comparison isn't "Dawnpipe vs Rosie" — it's
"Dawnpipe vs Rosie **plus** a $299–500/mo lead-gen service, plus the time to
run it."

**The one-line version for the phone:**

> "There are cheaper things that *answer*. There's nothing at any price that
> answers your phone **and** fills your pipeline. You're not buying minutes —
> you're buying a front desk that works both directions."

## Word-for-word responses

**"Rosie is $149 for 1,000 minutes."**
> "It is, and if all you want is answering, Rosie's fine. Here's the question
> though: where's next month's work coming from? Dawnpipe answers your phone
> — and between calls it finds you 200 potential customers a month and writes
> each one a personal email you approve. Rosie plus a lead-gen service plus
> someone to run it costs more than $499, and you'd be managing three things
> instead of zero."

**"Why 500 minutes when they give 1,000?"**
> "Five hundred minutes is roughly 150 to 250 answered calls a month — more
> than a single-location shop actually gets. And here's the part that matters:
> when you do run out, it pauses. It never bills you a cent more. Half the
> companies on your comparison list make their real money on overage; we
> don't have overage."

**"Will people really pay $499?"** *(your own doubt — here's the frame)*
> One recovered job pays for the month. Ask any owner what a job is worth —
> $300 to $3,000 in the trades — then ask how many calls went to voicemail
> last week. The product doesn't have to be cheap; it has to be obviously
> cheaper than the jobs they're losing. That's why the demo line is the whole
> pitch: they *hear* the thing that would have caught those calls.

**"What does Dawnpipe do that genuinely nobody else does?"** — the checklist:
1. **Outbound + inbound in one product.** Finds customers, writes to them,
   answers when they call. No competitor on the table bundles this.
2. **Instant AI call-backs** to anyone who fills the request form or replies
   YES — legally, with consent on file. Speed-to-lead in about 60 seconds.
3. **No overage, ever.** Allowances pause; they never bill. Ruby, Dialzara,
   Smith.ai and My AI Front Desk all monetise overage.
4. **You approve every outbound email.** Nothing sends without the owner.
5. **Repeat-caller memory, Spanish, emergency waterfall with a heads-up
   text** — table stakes plus, but say them; most rivals miss at least one.

## What NOT to say

- Don't trash competitors by name to a prospect — compare categories
  ("answering-only services"), not brands, unless they raise one first.
- Don't quote a competitor price you haven't verified this month.
- Don't promise text confirmations yet (carrier registration pending).
- Don't invent customers or results. "You'd be one of the first" + the demo
  line + the free week is the honest close, and it works.
