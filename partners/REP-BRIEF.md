# Rep brief — everything you need on one page

Print it. Keep it next to the phone.

---

## What you're selling, in one sentence

An AI receptionist that answers the calls a small contractor misses, asks
what's wrong, and books the job into their calendar — for $499/month, with
seven days free.

## Who to call

**Best fit:** US home-service and personal-service businesses, 1–15
employees, single location, **where the owner is also the person answering
the phone.** In order: plumbing, HVAC, electrical, garage door, roofing, auto
repair, then salons / barbershops / med spas.

**How to spot one that's already bleeding** — ranked by how strong the signal is:

1. **Google reviews in the last year that say "never called me back", "no
   answer", "left three voicemails."** This is the pitch handed to you in
   writing. Read it back to them.
2. They advertise **24-hour or emergency service** — but call after 5pm and it
   goes to voicemail. *Test it yourself before you dial.*
3. 20+ reviews (so they have real call volume) but **no online booking**.
4. Running **Google Ads or Angi/Thumbtack** — they're already paying for calls
   they aren't answering. That makes the ROI conversation arithmetic instead
   of theory.
5. No receptionist and no office extension listed.

**Skip:** multi-location, franchises with a corporate call center, anyone with
a full-time office manager, anyone under 10 reviews.

**When to call:** 7:30–9:00am or 4:00–6:00pm local. Before the first job or
after the last one.

**How to call:** yourself, from your own phone, to a business landline.
Scrub against the DNC registry. **Never an autodialer, never an AI voice, never
a prerecorded message.** That is the one rule that ends the relationship.

## The opener

> "Hi, is this the owner? Good — this is [YOUR NAME] with Dawnpipe, and I'm
> not selling you leads. One question and I'll get out of your way: last
> Tuesday at 2pm, when you were under a truck and the phone rang — who picked
> up?"
>
> *(stop talking. let them answer.)*
>
> "Right. That one went to voicemail, and that customer called the next guy.
> We put an AI receptionist on your line that answers around the clock, asks
> what's wrong, and books the job into your calendar. It's $499 a month, seven
> days free, cancel in one click.
>
> **Do you have sixty seconds to hear it? Hang up and call [DEMO NUMBER] right
> now — talk to it like you're a customer with a burst pipe. Then call me
> back and tell me what you think.**"

That last move is the whole pitch. You are not describing software; you are
handing them the product. Most people who actually make that call come back
sold, or come back with a real objection you can answer.

## The three objections you will hear

**1. "It's a robot. My customers will hate it."**

> "That's the right thing to worry about, and it's exactly why I want you to
> call it instead of taking my word. It says it's an AI in the first sentence
> — we don't hide it — and it only says what *you* tell it: your prices, your
> hours, your services. If it doesn't know something, it says so and takes a
> message. It never makes something up to your customer.
>
> And the comparison isn't the AI versus you. It's the AI versus **voicemail**,
> because that's what's answering right now."

**2. "$499 is a lot."**

> "What's one job worth to you? [let them answer — it's usually $300–$3,000]
> So this pays for itself if it catches **one** call a month you'd have lost.
> You're missing more than one a week.
>
> Compare it to the alternative: an answering service is around $300 a month
> and gives you a stranger reading a script who can't book anything. A
> part-time person is $2,000+ and goes home at five. This one works nights,
> weekends and holidays, and it books straight into your calendar."

**3. "I want to think about it."**

> "Course. But there's nothing to think about until you've heard it — so let's
> not decide anything today. Seven days free, card on file so we know you're a
> real business, nothing charged until day eight, and you cancel in one click
> before then and pay nothing.
>
> The only thing you're deciding today is whether it's worth **one week** of
> letting it answer the calls you're currently missing. What's your number?"

## What you may and may not say

**Read the Approved Claims sheet you signed.** The short version:

✅ **You may say:** it answers 24/7 in a natural voice · it books into their
calendar · it says it's an AI · it only says what they told it · it transfers
emergencies to them · it records and transcribes every call · it speaks
Spanish · it recognises repeat callers · 500 minutes + 200 leads on Front Desk
· 7 days free, cancel in one click, no contract, no setup fee, no overage
charges.

❌ **You may not say:** anything about AI cold calling or auto-dialing (we do
not sell it, and it's illegal for this product) · any ROI, percentage, or
dollar figure as a *promise* · how many customers we have · any testimonial,
case study, or "trusted by" claim — **we have none** · anything about text
messages to their customers, **which are not live yet** (see below) ·
anything a competitor does that you haven't verified.

### One thing that is NOT live yet — do not sell it

**Text messaging is switched off.** Booking confirmations by text, missed-call
text-backs, and emergency alert texts are all built but not yet enabled while
we complete carrier registration. **Do not mention texts, do not promise them,
do not use them as a closing feature.** If a prospect asks directly:

> "Texting is built but not switched on yet — we're finishing the carrier
> registration every US business has to do. It'll come on automatically and
> your price won't change. Right now the AI answers, qualifies, and books; the
> booking lands in your calendar and you'll see it on your phone."

Selling a feature that doesn't work is the fastest way to a refund, a
chargeback, and a reversed commission.

## Your closing asset

**The 7-day free trial on their own number.** The demo line earns you the
conversation; the trial closes it. Get them signed up while you're on the
phone — walk them through it — because "I'll do it tonight" is where deals go
to die.

**Say this while they're signing up:** *"Use code [YOUR LAST NAME] in the
referral box, or here — I'll text you my link right now."* Either one credits
the sale to you. If they somehow use neither, tell the founder and the account
gets assigned to you anyway, with back-pay.

---

## Honest context, so you're not surprised

We have **no customers yet**. You are early. That means no logos, no
references, no case studies — and it also means the market is wide open and
the accounts you sign are yours at 30% for two years.

If a prospect asks "who else uses this?", the only honest answer is the one
you give: *"You'd be one of the first. That's why you're getting my phone
number and the founder's, and why there's a free week with no card charged."*

Do not invent a customer. Ever. One fabricated reference costs more than every
deal you'll close this month.
