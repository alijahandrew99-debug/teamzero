## Headline 1 — the open relay is still live, and the backlog is still the real finding

I re-derived this instead of citing it, because a month-old "still broken" claim
deserves re-checking.

`main:lib/smtp.js:105`:

```js
await talk(socket, `RCPT TO:<${msg.to}>`, [250, 251]);
```

and `main:lib/smtp.js:113`: `` `To: <${msg.to}>` ``.

**There is no CRLF guard on `msg.to` anywhere on the path.** I checked both ends:

- `lib/smtp.js` has no sanitiser for it. `encodeHeader` (`:76`) covers only
  `fromName` and `subject`, and it neutralises CR/LF there incidentally by
  base64-wrapping anything outside printable ASCII. The envelope fields have no
  such cover. Line `:83`'s `replace(/\r?\n/g, '\r\n')` is body dot-stuffing, not
  address validation.
- `db.addLeads` (`main:lib/db.js:407-425`) applies `.trim()` to the address and
  nothing else — no shape check, no rejection. `.trim()` strips *surrounding*
  whitespace, so an **interior** CRLF survives intact.

So `a@b.com>\r\nRCPT TO:<victim@x.com` imported from a CSV reaches `RCPT TO:`
unmodified and sends a second envelope recipient; the same bytes in the `To:`
header can close `DATA` and begin a new message. On accounts using "let Dawnpipe
send for you" it leaves Dawnpipe's own system mailbox, so the blacklisting lands
on Dawnpipe's sending domain — AUDIT.md §15's existential risk, reproduced.
**Confidence: high.** Actively exploitable by any user who can import a lead.

The fix exists twice over and neither has merged:
`audit/2026-09-16-smtp-crlf-injection`, and `keeper/2026-10-01-consolidated-critical-fixes`
(dated 6 days ago), which bundles the SMTP guard with `db.js`, `server.js` and
`views/app.html` changes. **The consolidated branch is the one to merge** — it is
the most recent and carries the most, and its guard is written carefully enough to
avoid being mistaken for a hard bounce by `suppression.isHardBounce`.

## Finding 1 (NEW) — one call's minutes billed once per duplicate callback, unbounded

**Confidence: certain.** Reproduced end to end against the running server with
real signed webhooks, fixed, then re-proved. Checked against all 27 open branches
and all six prior reports for a duplicate — see "relationship to prior work".

### Where

`server.js:1877` (in-memory path) and `server.js:1858-1862` (fallback), with
`lib/plans.js:357-372` doing the arithmetic.

### What is wrong

`/voice/status` bills `Math.ceil(CallDuration / 60)` on **every** delivery and
records nothing about what a given `CallSid` was already charged. Twilio delivers
status callbacks *at least* once, so a redelivery charges the whole call again —
once per delivery, no ceiling.

The two paths do not protect each other; they chain:

1. First delivery: `voice.getCall(sid)` finds the live call, bills it, acks, then
   `voice.endCall(sid)` (`server.js:1951`) deletes it from the in-memory Map
   (`lib/voice.js:314-323`).
2. Any later delivery for that SID: the call is now gone, so it falls into the
   `!call` fallback — the branch whose comment says it is for "a deploy mid-call
   wipes the in-memory record" — which bills the **full duration again**.

### Why last week's "clean" verdict missed it

2026-09-30 recorded under *Checked and clean*:

> **No double-billing of voice minutes.** […] the deploy-mid-call fallback
> (`server.js:1858`) only fires when the in-memory call is already gone, so the
> two paths cannot both bill.

The premise is right and the conclusion does not follow. The two paths cannot both
bill *within one request* — but `endCall` makes the call "already gone" **as a
result of** the first delivery, so across two deliveries both paths bill. The
verdict reasoned about one webhook in isolation; the bug only exists in the
sequence of two. Recording the shape of the error because it is an easy one to
repeat: *on a retry-capable path, "these two branches are mutually exclusive" has
to be checked across deliveries, not within one.*

### What provokes the retry

Billing happens ~70 lines before `res.end()`, with a synchronous rewrite of
`calls.json` plus two more full reads of it in between. Measured at the
3000-record cap `saveCall` enforces (`lib/db.js:188`): **9.1 MB file, ~24 ms per
read, ~58 ms read-modify-write, ~105 ms of blocked event loop per
`/voice/status`.** That is the bill-to-ack window, and three ordinary things land
in it:

- A burst of simultaneous call endings → late ack → Twilio retries (its webhook
  timeout is 15 s; ~142 queued webhooks at 105 ms each exhausts it).
- **A Render deploy landing in the window** → response dropped after the bill was
  written → Twilio retries → the restarted process has no in-memory call, so the
  retry takes the double-billing fallback. `main` auto-deploys, so this is
  routine.
- Twilio's at-least-once semantics generally; it does not promise exactly-once and
  its own docs tell consumers to be idempotent.

This is finding 3 of last week's report (blocked event loop) acting as the trigger
for this one. They are the same story told from two ends.

### What it costs the customer

Precisely, because it is **not** a card overcharge: there is no overage billing
(`partners/APPROVED-CLAIMS.md:45` — "No overage charges, ever — when an allowance
runs out it pauses"). The damage is that a paying customer's **included** minutes
drain faster than their real usage, and when the allowance reaches zero
`plans.voiceAllowed` fails and `server.js:1208` drops their line to message-only
for the rest of the month. It degrades gracefully — it does not hang up on their
callers, which is good design, and it is why this is "the product quietly stops
doing the thing they bought" rather than "the line goes dead". On a $399/mo plan
whose promise is that calls get answered and jobs booked, with the owner's only
clue being an activity-log line, that is still severe. It also corrupts the one
number that says whether Front Desk's ~$71/customer margin is real.

### Proof

Three byte-identical deliveries of one 120-second call against unpatched `main`,
through the same signed-webhook harness the repo's own suites use:

```
voiceMinutesUsed before any status callback: 0
after delivery #1 (120s = 2 min):           2
after delivery #2 (identical retry):        4
after delivery #3:                          6
```

### Fix — `audit/2026-10-07-voice-minute-double-bill`

The telling detail: **lead metering already had this guard and minute metering
never got it.** `lib/agents.js:495` charges only `if (!lead.metered)` and stamps
the lead. Calls now get the same treatment — persist `billedMin` on the call
record and charge only the difference, so an identical retry costs nothing while a
later callback reporting a longer call still tops the minutes up.

- `lib/db.js` — new `callBySid(sid)` to read back what a SID was charged.
- `server.js` — both billing paths charge `minsToBill - alreadyBilled`.
- `test-voicebilling.js` — new suite: bills once, retries bill nothing, a longer
  duration tops up, and the deploy-wiped-memory case still bills exactly once.
  **4 of its 7 checks fail without the change** (verified by stashing the fix).

`node --check` clean on all three. All five existing suites still pass — 96
checks: spam 18, voiceprofiles 17, reps 37, assistant 15, sending-mode 9.

One honest cost: this adds a third full read of `calls.json` to `/voice/status`
(~24 ms at the cap), making last week's finding 3 marginally worse. Worth it to
stop an unbounded mischarge, and a further reason to act on the hot-path writes.

### Relationship to prior work — read this before merging

`audit/2026-08-26-voice-status-fallback-billing` **touches the same lines for a
different bug**: the fallback bills calls where nobody spoke or that were flagged
spam, which the live path correctly excludes. It does not address idempotency —
it still bills `Math.ceil(dur0/60)` unconditionally per delivery.

**The two are complementary and will conflict textually.** Both are needed: mine
stops the same call being billed twice, that one stops a robocall being billed at
all. Merging either alone leaves half the bug. Combined, the fallback wants both
guards — the spoke/spam check from 08-26 gating whether `mins` is non-zero, and
`billedMin` gating how much of it is new. Whoever merges should take them
together; I have deliberately not rebased onto that branch, because silently
folding in someone else's unreviewed fix would hide it from review.

Recorded so these are not mistaken for new work, and so the branch that fixes each
is easy to find.

- **Global instead of per-tenant history caps.** `lib/db.js:188`
  (`calls.slice(-3000)`) and `:361` (`smsThreads.slice(-2000)`) cap across *all*
  tenants, so the busiest account evicts everyone else's records — ~15 calls each
  at COST.md's 200-customer horizon, with `callSid` references on appointments
  left dangling. I found this independently, then found
  **`keeper/2026-09-05-per-account-retention` already fixes it** with a
  `capPerAccountOldestFirst` helper mirroring the existing `capPerAccount`
  (`lib/db.js:549`) that `logActivity` already uses. Nothing more needed from me:
  **merge that branch.**
- **Synchronous whole-collection IO on the voice hot path.** Last week's finding 3.
  Confirmed independently; my 9.1 MB / ~105 ms per `/voice/status` agrees with its
  measurements in shape, and it is the trigger for my finding 1 above. Unchanged
  conclusion: architecture, not a patch.
- **`plans.consume` trusting a caller-supplied snapshot.** The 2026-09-02 finding.
  Last week recorded the counter paths as guarded, and for voice that is right —
  `server.js:1874` re-reads on the line before the synchronous consume. One
  residual gap, low severity: in `lib/agents.js`, `acct` is read at `:473`,
  `await outboundDraft(...)` runs at `:482`, and `plans.consume(acct, 1, false)` at
  `:495` still uses the pre-await snapshot. The per-iteration re-read makes the
  *sequential* loop correct, but two concurrent runs for one account (two
  profiles, or the night shift overlapping a manual run) both read `leadsUsed: 10`
  and both write `11`. `lead.metered` stops a lead being charged twice but not a
  lost increment. Direction of error is **under**-counting, so the customer
  benefits — a margin leak, not customer harm. Not fixed: making metering stricter
  on a live product is a product call, and the robust version is three lines in
  `plans.js` (re-read by id at the top of both `consume` and
  `consumeVoiceMinutes`, so the read-modify-write has no yield in it regardless of
  caller discipline).

## Checked and clean this run

- **Twilio webhook signature verification — solid.** `lib/voice.js:156-163` uses
  `crypto.timingSafeEqual` behind a length check, fails closed when
  `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN` is absent, and `server.js:1155` applies it to **every**
  `/voice/*` path before any dispatch. I went looking specifically for an
  unauthenticated replay of `/voice/status`, which would have turned finding 1
  into a remote way to zero any customer's minutes. Properly closed.
- **Deriving the signed URL from `x-forwarded-proto`/`-host`
  (`server.js:1150-1152`) is not a forgery hole** — an attacker controls the base
  string but still needs the auth token to produce a matching HMAC.
- **`/voice/recording` (`server.js:1759-1766`) is idempotent** — recording
  metadata only, no billing.
- **Spam/screening billing exclusions hold**, including after my change:
  `callerSpoke`, `call.spamCall` and the blocked-caller `<Reject>` all still
  bypass metering, and the existing suite's "spam call minutes are NOT billed"
  check passes against my branch.
